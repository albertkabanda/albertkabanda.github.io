## Research

My research uses seismology to investigate the structure, deformation, and evolution of the crust and upper mantle, with a particular focus on active continental rifting. I combine observations from earthquakes and ambient seismic noise with seismic imaging, inverse methods, and computational analysis to understand how continental lithosphere responds as it is stretched, weakened, and modified during rifting.

### Continental Rifting and Lithospheric Structure

A major focus of my research is the **East African Rift System**, particularly the northern Western Branch in Uganda and the broader western rift region. This part of the rift provides a natural setting for investigating how continental extension varies between regions where magmatism is important and regions where deformation is dominated primarily by tectonic processes.

I use seismic observations to characterize crustal and lithospheric structure beneath the rift and surrounding Precambrian terranes. My work examines how variations in crustal thickness, seismic velocity structure, and major subsurface boundaries relate to rift segmentation, inherited tectonic structure, magmatism, and the early stages of continental breakup.

### Receiver Functions and Crustal Imaging

I use **teleseismic receiver functions** to investigate discontinuities within the crust and upper mantle. Receiver functions isolate seismic energy converted at subsurface velocity contrasts and provide strong constraints on the depths of interfaces such as the Moho.

My work combines receiver-function analysis with techniques including **H–κ stacking, waveform modeling and inversion, and common-conversion-point imaging**. I use these complementary approaches to investigate spatial variations in crustal structure beneath seismic stations and across active rift zones, while evaluating uncertainties associated with sedimentary basins, complex crustal structure, and lateral variations in seismic velocity.

### Ambient Noise and Surface-Wave Tomography

Another major component of my research uses **ambient seismic noise** to extract surface waves between seismic stations. By cross-correlating long records of continuous seismic data, I investigate Rayleigh-wave dispersion and use these measurements to constrain variations in shear-wave velocity beneath the study region.

I am particularly interested in how the **directionality and seasonality of ambient seismic noise** affect empirical Green's functions and surface-wave measurements. Because seismic noise recorded in East Africa is influenced by energetic ocean-generated sources surrounding the African continent, understanding variations in noise-source illumination is important for producing reliable dispersion measurements and seismic images.

I use these observations to develop surface-wave datasets for **ambient-noise tomography and crustal-to-lithospheric imaging**, and to integrate surface-wave constraints with receiver functions for improved interpretation of subsurface structure.

### Upper-Mantle and Lithospheric Imaging

My broader research interests extend from the crust into the **continental lithosphere and upper mantle**. I am interested in determining how mantle structure, lithospheric thickness, inherited tectonic fabrics, and deeper mantle processes influence where continental rifts develop and how they evolve.

I am particularly interested in integrating receiver functions and surface-wave imaging with **teleseismic body-wave tomography** to investigate structure from the crust through the lithosphere and into the deeper upper mantle. These complementary observations can help distinguish crustal deformation from deeper lithospheric and mantle processes associated with continental rifting.

### Computational Geophysics

My research relies heavily on scientific computing and quantitative data analysis. I develop and adapt computational workflows for processing large seismic datasets, measuring seismic-wave properties, performing inversions, evaluating model resolution, and visualizing Earth structure.

I primarily work in **Python** together with established seismological software and high-performance computing environments. My broader computational interests include **inverse problems, optimization, statistical analysis, machine learning, time-series analysis, and reproducible scientific workflows**. I am especially interested in using modern computational methods to extract more information from increasingly large seismic datasets while maintaining physically interpretable models of the Earth.
